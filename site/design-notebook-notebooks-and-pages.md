<h1>Notebooks and Pages</h1>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A notebook is an organization-scoped folder for one game's design. Its pages are the Game Design Documents inside it, and the tree on the Design page's left side is how you move between them. This page covers creating, ordering, renaming, archiving, and recovering notebooks and their pages.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="creating-a-notebook">Creating a notebook</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Choose <strong>new notebook</strong> in the tree's header, give it a name and an optional description, and create it. The notebook appears in your organization's list immediately. The search box above the list filters notebooks by name or description.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Every item in the tree also has a details affordance (the ⓘ on hover): it shows the item's metadata and opens its editable agent instructions, the standing guidance agents receive whenever they work on that item. Notebooks, pages, files, assets, and tickets all carry instructions. See <a href="design-notebook-agents">Working with agents</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="creating-pages">Creating pages</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Under a notebook's <strong>GDDs</strong> heading, <strong>+ new</strong> creates an empty page with the title you give it, and <strong>import .md</strong> loads a Markdown file from your computer. An import takes its title from the file's first heading unless you type one, and lands in the editor as revision 1 with its section structure parsed.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A page's title is independent of any heading inside it: rename the page from its row's context menu without touching the Markdown.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="the-page-order">The page order</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The tree lists a notebook's pages in the notebook's own display order, the same for every viewer, so a notebook reads in a deliberate order such as Concept, then Systems, then Levels, then Appendices. Pages that have never been reordered list in creation order; new pages append at the end.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Reorder pages two ways: drag a page's row onto another page (a ⠿ handle appears on hover, and the drop target shows an insertion line), or right-click a page and choose <strong>Move up</strong> or <strong>Move down</strong>. The tree updates immediately and the order persists per notebook.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="renaming-archiving-restoring-purging">Renaming, archiving, restoring, purging</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Right-click a page for its menu: <strong>Rename…</strong> changes the title, and <strong>Delete (archive)</strong> removes the page from the tree. Deletion is an archive: the page, its notes, statuses, and history are all retained and recoverable. Enable <strong>Show archived</strong> under the notebook's GDD list to see archived pages, then <strong>restore</strong> one from its row. Right-click an archived notebook the same way to restore it.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Delete permanently</strong> (purge) appears for archived items and removes everything for real: a purged page takes its sections, notes, and statuses with it, and a purged notebook takes its pages, files, asset entries, and tickets. Purging is reserved to owners and admins and asks for confirmation naming exactly what will be lost.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The tree marks a page with an amber dot while it has unsaved changes, so you can see at a glance where work is in flight.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
