<h1>Followers</h1>

<!-- wp:separator -->
<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity"/>
<!-- /wp:separator -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":6} -->
<h6 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-elements-allows-online-user-profiles-to-build-a-following-which-is-a-more-loosely-defined-relationship-between-two-profiles-than-friends">Elements allows online User profiles to build a following, which is a more loosely defined relationship between two profiles than Friends.</h6>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Followers represent a social relationship between two Profiles. Unlike <a href="friends">Friends</a>, two <a href="users-and-profiles">Profiles</a> make a Follower/Followee relationship and the relationship can be much more loosely defined.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Followers rely only on Profiles, and therefore all information in the Follower/Followee relationship is public information. The APIs dealing with Followers return <a href="users-and-profiles">Profile</a> instances.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Followers are meant to allow for casual relationships among users of a particular application. Because they operate strictly on Profiles and public information, there is no need for modeling Following as a mutual relationship. Either User may follow or unfollow others as they see fit.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
